WHO I AM ↗

I have a Master's in Advertising, which is a way of saying I was trained to make people care about things they didn't know they needed to care about. Turns out, I'm quite good at it.

For the past 5 years I've worked across luxury, entertainment, FMCG, education, fashion eCommerce, real estate, hospitality, and jewellery, with brands that needed people to not just notice them, but believe in them. Because strategy that doesn't build belief doesn't build anything.

My process involves a lot of thinking out loud, several cups of chai, and an unreasonable amount of time spent understanding the audience before touching the brief. My colleagues call it thorough. I call it necessary, and that gap is where I work.

WHAT I DO ↗

I use data to find the real question, then I use storytelling to answer it.

Most strategies fail not because the targeting was wrong or the budget was thin. They fail because nobody stopped to ask what the brand actually means to the person on the other side. I build strategies that start there. Brand narratives, content systems, CRM journeys, all of it designed around intent, not just reach.

The work only counts if it changes how someone thinks, feels, or decides.

WHY I DO IT ↓

I got into strategy because I kept noticing the same thing, brands spending energy in the wrong direction. More content, more platforms, more noise. Nobody stopping to ask whether any of it was actually landing with the person it was meant for.

I wanted to be the person who asks that question before the work starts. Five years in, I still think it's the most important question in the room.

01. Luxury Hospitality · Heritage Brand

How contextual storytelling drove a 60% jump in qualified leads.

Ahilya Fort is a heritage hospitality brand rooted in Maheshwar, with a sister property in Goa.

⬚
Hero Image · Video placeholder
The Challenge
Despite a massive legacy, the digital presence was attracting the wrong guest.

People were evaluating Ahilya Fort like a standard luxury hotel, comparing room rates, reading amenity lists, treating it like any other premium stay. The deep cultural significance wasn't landing. The soulful heritage experience wasn't being communicated.

The bookings were low-intent. The guests were arriving with the wrong expectations. And the Fort, with everything it stands for, was being reduced to a transaction.

The Insight
The problem wasn't a lack of interest. It was a lack of context.

In the premium segment, if you don't sell the history, you're just selling a room, and that makes you a commodity. The Fort's legacy was never the backdrop. It was the product. We needed to stop selling rooms and start selling stewardship.

"Context is what converts. And context was entirely missing."
What We Did
Stopped leading with amenities. Started leading with meaning.
01. Content Reframing
Every touchpoint rewritten around heritage

Not because it sounded premium, because it was true. The Fort's story was always the product. We just made sure it arrived before the room rate did.

02. Behavioural CRM
Three traveller mindsets. Three distinct conversations.

The Culture Seeker, the Heritage Traveller, the Mindful Explorer. Each received communication that spoke directly to why they specifically would care, not a generic luxury pitch.

03. Visual Body Language
Stopped broadcasting. Started documenting.

Artisan detail, living history, the texture of a place that hasn't forgotten who it is. Content designed to attract the right eyes, and quietly repel the wrong ones.

What Happened
60%
Jump in qualified leads. The enquiries that came in weren't asking about price. They were asking about the experience. That's the shift.
3×
Email open rates, from 0.7% to 2.1%. Hyper-relevant segmentation meant every message landed with the right person at the right moment.
What I Learned
01
Filtering the wrong audience out is as valuable as pulling the right one in. Volume is not the metric. Quality of intent is.
02
In luxury, the moment you lead with what something means rather than what it costs, you stop competing on a metric you were never going to win anyway.

02 Luxury Food & Beverage

When the room has soul, the digital presence should too.

Circle Sixty Nine is a neighbourhood bistro in Worli, Mumbai, Bistro & Bar in Worli, Mumbai.

The Challenge
The challenge wasn't awareness. It was conversion from interest to action.

Digital visibility was consistent, people were seeing Circle Sixty Nine online. But that discovery wasn't reliably translating into chairs filled on-ground. There was a friction gap.

The digital storytelling was episodic, it showed food, but it didn't sell the vibe or the occasion. Visual storytelling lacked a consistent creative direction. WhatsApp existed as a channel without a defined role in driving behaviour. The space had soul. The content didn't show it.

The Insight
Digital communication sets expectations. The experience delivers conversion.

In F&B, digital communication sets the table. The experience serves the meal. If the table isn't set right, if someone can't feel what kind of place this is before they arrive, guests never show up.

"You can't build a community by announcing yourself. You build it by showing people they already belong."
What We Did
Made the culture visible.
01. Visual Language
Off the plate, into the room

Rebuilt creative direction around the full life of the space, the art on the walls, people mid-conversation, the light at a certain hour. Content that said this is the kind of place, and the kind of crowd, before anyone had to ask.

02. Events as Invitations
A door worth walking through

Events stopped being announcements and became invitations. Specific, considered, worth rearranging your Saturday for. Each one a reason for the community to gather, and for someone new to realise they'd found their people.

03. WhatsApp as Community Thread
Warm, direct, closing

Not broadcasting, connecting. A direct line that turned curiosity into presence, presence into regulars, and regulars into community. Every message had one job: make showing up feel easy.

What Happened
100%
Footfall doubled during event-led campaigns. The people who came weren't just customers, they were exactly the audience Circle Sixty Nine had always been for. The room started matching the vision.
What I Learned
01
For a space built on culture, the strategy is the creative. There's no separating brand from community from content, they're the same thing expressed in different places.
02
Neighbourhood brands don't need reach. They need resonance. When the digital presence finally reflected what Circle Sixty Nine actually stood for, the right people found it. They always do, they just needed to be shown the door.

03. General Entertainment · Media

They weren't running out of content. They were running out of reasons for people to stay.

Colors TV Middle East is a general entertainment channel serving a culturally diverse regional audience.

⬚
Hero Image · Video placeholder
The Challenge
The challenge was not content supply. It was retention and efficiency.

Despite a high volume of output, digital performance, particularly on YouTube, was not translating into sustained watch time or efficient media outcomes. Audiences were clicking into videos but not staying long enough to drive watch time or justify rising CPVs.

Existing content formats were misaligned with regional consumption behaviour, leading to inefficient use of both content and media spend.

The Insight
Content was not failing. Formats were.

Stories had cultural relevance, but the way they were packaged did not encourage audiences to stay engaged. The opportunity lay in optimising how content was presented, not increasing how much was published.

"The highest-leverage intervention is often not more content, but better packaged content."
What We Did
Stopped optimising for output. Started optimising for retention.
01. Hook Optimisation
Win the first eight seconds

Rebuilt formats around stronger entry points. Because if you lose them in the opening, nothing else matters. The hook isn't a creative choice, it's a strategic one.

02. Format Testing
Built for this audience, not borrowed from another

Tested meme-led and character-driven formats calibrated to regional behaviour. Not adapted from generic best practice. Designed specifically for how the Middle East audience actually engages.

03. Unified Platform Logic
Social creates curiosity. YouTube earns the time.

Restructured the platform relationship so each channel had a defined role. Validated everything organically before amplification. Spend follows signal, not the other way around.

What Happened
81%
Increase in YouTube watch time. The content hadn't changed, the packaging had.
70%
Growth in social views. More people entering, more people staying.
−30%
Reduction in CPV. The media got more efficient because the content earned it first.
What I Learned
01
The most expensive thing you can do in media is amplify content that hasn't proved itself yet. Organic validation isn't a step you skip when you're in a hurry, it's the step that makes the paid work worth doing.
02
Spend follows signal. Always. When the content earns attention organically, paid amplification multiplies it. When it doesn't, paid just accelerates the waste.

04. Luxury Furniture · Mumbai

How we used AI-powered content to drive footfall for a brand the internet had never met.

Red Blue & Yellow is a Mumbai-based luxury furniture brand with over 20 years of design heritage, known for premium solid wood craftsmanship and a distinctly modern Indian aesthetic. Featured in Elle Decor and Architectural Digest.

⬚
Hero Image · Video placeholder
The Brief
A 20-year-old brand the internet had never met.

When the client came to us, they had one thing, static catalogue photos of furniture. No videos. No production budget. No digital presence. Just a brand with two decades of design heritage and no way to show it.

We could have waited for a shoot. We didn't.

The Concept
Every piece of furniture at RBY has a story. We decided to tell it.

Instead of just showing the product, we wanted each Reel to reveal the inspiration behind it. The furniture wasn't just an object, it was an idea made physical. That became the entire content framework.

What We Did
No script. No shoot. Just creativity and the right tools.
01. AI-Powered Production
Brought static images to life

With no video assets available, we used AI tools to animate the catalogue photography, the concept and creative direction was Miloni's, the execution built with her designer. Constraints became the creative brief.

02. Story-First Content
Inspiration behind every piece

Each Reel was built around the design story, where the idea came from, what it references, what it means. Content that gave the audience something to feel, not just something to scroll past.

03. Platform Strategy
Social as the discovery engine

Built the brand's digital presence from zero, establishing a visual language, content rhythm, and audience on platforms the brand had never existed on before.

What Happened
40%
Increase in footfall directly attributed to social media. A brand with no digital presence was now driving real-world traffic.
20 yrs
Of design heritage finally reaching a wide audience. The brand's best pieces saw significantly higher demand.
What I Learned
01
Constraints are a brief. When you don't have what you need, you adapt, and sometimes what you build under those constraints is more interesting than what a full production budget would have produced.
02
AI isn't a compromise. Used with a clear creative direction, it's a tool that makes things possible that weren't possible before. The concept was still the work. The tool just executed it.

Ahilya Fort · Heritage Campaign

Turning a 300th birth anniversary into a cultural movement.

Ahilya Fort is a heritage hospitality brand rooted in Maheshwar. The 300th birth anniversary of Ahilyabai Holkar was an open brief, develop the full campaign strategy, content, and collateral to bring it to life.

⬚
Image · Video placeholder
The Brief
An open brief and a significant cultural moment.

The brief was open, develop the full campaign strategy, content, and collateral to bring a significant cultural moment to life across digital and on-ground.

The Strategic Decision
A cultural tribute, not a promotional moment.

The birth anniversary was positioned as a tribute to Ahilyabai Holkar's legacy as a ruler, craft patron, and community builder, not as a brand marketing opportunity. This framing extended the campaign's relevance far beyond Ahilya Fort's existing audience and reinforced the brand's identity as a genuine steward of living heritage.

"Heritage storytelling, executed with cultural coherence, drives commercial outcomes across digital and physical touchpoints simultaneously."
The Execution
01. Organic Social
Cultural storytelling at scale

Content built around the historical and cultural significance of Ahilyabai Holkar, driving strong follower growth, engagement, shares, and saves well beyond the account's existing reach.

02. Godrej Activation
In collaboration with Rehwa Society

A three-day on-ground activation featuring the handloom cooperative associated with Ahilya Fort, bringing Ahilyabai Holkar's craft legacy into a contemporary cultural context. Full event collateral developed.

03. Raw Mango Activation
Curated collection, cultural retail

A second three-day activation featuring a curated collection at Raw Mango, extending the campaign into a high-relevance retail context and reaching a new, culturally engaged audience.

The Result
20%
Community growth on organic social, with strong follower growth, engagement, shares and saves beyond existing reach.
2×
Sell-out activations, strong saree sales across both the Godrej and Raw Mango events, demonstrating commercial impact alongside cultural resonance.

Circle Sixty Nine · Weekly Cultural IP

Thai Sabai, fully booked every Thursday for three months.

A recurring dining experience built to give Mumbai's food audience a specific, time-bound reason to visit Circle Sixty Nine every week.

⬚
Image · Video placeholder
The Brief
Give Mumbai's food audience a specific reason to show up every Thursday.

Circle Sixty Nine needed a recurring dining experience, not a one-off event, but a consistent weekly format that built audience habit rather than generated a single booking spike.

The Approach
01. The Concept
A weekly cultural moment

Thai Sabai was positioned around authentic Thai cuisine, underloved and underrepresented in Mumbai's dining landscape. The recurring Thursday format was designed to build habit, not just bookings.

02. The Channel Strategy
Each platform had a defined role

Instagram Reels and Carousels built cultural desire. Stories drove weekly urgency. WhatsApp converted interest into confirmed reservations. District extended reach into Mumbai's broader food-engaged audience.

03. On-Ground Storytelling
The campaign continued at the table

A branded origami fortune teller carrying Thai phrases and menu cues ensured cultural storytelling ran through the physical dining experience, generating organic sharing that extended the campaign beyond owned channels.

Circle Sixty Nine · Cultural Evening

Mahjong Night, a cultural evening that sold out entirely.

A single-night cultural experience designed to prove that Circle Sixty Nine's event-led model could extend beyond cuisine into broader cultural territory.

⬚
Image · Video placeholder
The Brief
Prove the event model works beyond cuisine.

Circle Sixty Nine had established itself as a community-driven space. Mahjong Night was conceived to test whether that cultural programming could extend into broader territory, a game, a social ritual, an evening built around experience rather than just a meal.

The Approach
01. Cultural First
A cultural evening. A dining event second.

Mahjong anchored the social experience. The game gave people a reason to be there beyond the food, turning dinner into an evening with intention.

02. Coherent Storytelling
The theme ran through every touchpoint

Dishes were plated to directly reference Mahjong tiles, ensuring cultural storytelling ran from the digital content through to the on-ground dining experience. Nothing felt disconnected.

03. Channel Roles
Discovery and conversion clearly separated

Instagram handled discovery and desire-building. WhatsApp drove direct conversion into confirmed bookings. Each channel had one job and did it.

The Result
100%
The event sold out entirely, reinforcing that cultural programming, executed with creative coherence from digital through to on-ground, consistently drives full-capacity results.
